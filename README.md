## What Should You Compare When Looking at Crypto Exchanges?

A crypto exchange is more than a screen with a Bitcoin price and a buy button. The important differences tend to appear after you deposit money and start using the platform regularly.

### 1. Trading fees

Most centralized exchanges use a maker-and-taker model.

- A **maker** order adds liquidity to the order book, usually by resting until another trader fills it.
- A **taker** order removes liquidity by matching an existing order, which is what normally happens with a market order.
- A limit order can be charged as either maker or taker, depending on how it executes.

OKX explains that fees are based on the actual execution of an order rather than simply whether the order was called “market” or “limit.” A limit order that fills immediately may still receive the taker rate.

Do not compare only the headline trading fee. Also check:

- Whether the fee applies to spot, margin, futures, or options
- Whether the rate changes by trading pair
- Whether volume is calculated over 30 days
- Whether the exchange charges a spread through a simple buy or convert function
- Withdrawal fees and minimum withdrawal amounts
- Funding fees for perpetual futures
- Liquidation fees and settlement fees

For example, OKX says that Convert and express Buy/Sell transactions show one quoted price instead of a separate order-book fee. The spread or service cost is incorporated into that quote, so it should not automatically be compared with a standard spot maker fee.

### 3. Regional access

This is one of the easiest details to overlook.

An exchange may be globally available while offering different products, fee schedules, payment methods, and derivatives access in the United States, European Economic Area, United Kingdom, or other regions. OKX explicitly states that fee information and product availability may vary by customer and region.

Before depositing funds, check:

1. Whether your country or state is supported
2. Which assets can be traded in your region
3. Whether derivatives are available
4. Which fiat currencies and payment methods are supported
5. Whether identity verification is required
6. Whether the advertised promotion applies to your account location

This matters more than finding a platform with the lowest fee on a comparison page. A low fee is not useful if the market, deposit method, or withdrawal route you need is unavailable.

## How the OKX Fee Structure Works in Real Use

The tier table is only one part of the cost calculation.

### Spot trading

For spot and margin trading, OKX describes the basic calculation as:

> Trading fee = fee rate × the amount of crypto bought or sold when the order is filled.

The fee may be deducted from the asset purchased or from the sale proceeds, depending on the transaction. The final fee can also differ from a rough estimate if an order fills in multiple parts or executes under different maker and taker conditions.

### Futures

Futures fees are calculated against the position value, not just the margin deposited.

If a trader opens a $20,000 position with $2,000 of margin at 10x leverage, the trading fee is based on the $20,000 position value. Leverage can therefore make the visible margin look smaller than the amount used in the fee calculation. Funding payments are separate from trading fees and can also affect the final result.

This is one reason beginners should avoid choosing an exchange based only on the lowest advertised futures fee. The product itself carries additional risks, including liquidation and funding costs.

### Options and spreads

OKX also publishes separate rules for options and spread trading. Options fees can depend on the contract structure and may be subject to minimum or maximum calculations. The exchange states that spread instruments use a fee rate equal to 50% of the applicable classic order-book rate for each leg.

These products are aimed at users who already understand derivatives mechanics. They are not simply cheaper versions of spot trading.
